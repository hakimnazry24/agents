# IEC 60601-1-2:2014 ed. 4.0 — Electromagnetic disturbances (key EM)

**Source:** checked against the full text of IEC 60601-1-2:2014 (Edition 4.0, 2014-02). Clause numbers are exact for that edition. Edition 4.1 (2020) is 4.0 plus Amendment 1; confirm clause numbers against 4.1 if the hospital or regulator cites it.

**In plain words:** medical electrical equipment must stay safe and keep doing its essential job when radio waves and electrical noise are around it, and must not send out so much of its own that it disturbs other devices. The maker has to think about these disturbances in its risk analysis, test the hardware, and tell users where the equipment may be used and how close radios may come.

**What the standard covers that matters for INNUTRIRE:**
- **Scope** (1.1): the standard applies to medical electrical equipment and medical electrical systems (equipment combined with other devices). Software running on ordinary IT equipment (3.10, "information technology equipment") is not medical electrical equipment on its own, so the first question is whether the standard applies at all, and that answer has to be written down. Existing hospital networks don't need testing, but their effects belong in risk management (Annex A, rationale for 1.1).
- **Risk-based approach** (4.1, Annex F): risks from foreseeable electromagnetic disturbances must be part of the risk management process, and compliance is checked by looking for those entries in the risk management file. Annex F names software effects such as a program crashing, looping or resetting (F.3), suggests software error recovery and fail-safe behaviour as controls (F.5.1), and says testing alone can never show the risk is acceptable.
- **Essential performance and pass/fail criteria** (8.1, Annex I): the maker decides in advance what counts as failure. Failure examples that fit software: a displayed number wrong enough to change treatment, stored settings changed, a reset to defaults, an intended function stopping (I.3.1).
- **Ordinary IT equipment inside a medical system** (4.2, 8.8, 7.1.3): it must meet its own EMC standards (CISPR 32 for multimedia and IT equipment). Extra testing to this standard is needed only if the risk analysis shows it could affect the system's safety. The equipment maker's declaration of conformity is acceptable evidence (Annex A, rationale for 4.2).
- **Radio equipment** (7.1.6): equipment approved under national radio rules can use that approval in place of some emission tests.
- **Instructions for use** (5.2.1.1): must state the environments the equipment suits and the places to avoid (a); what the essential function is and what users can expect if a disturbance degrades it (b); a warning against using it right next to or stacked on other equipment (c); the cables and accessories that matter and a warning against substitutes (d, e); and a warning to keep portable radio equipment at least 30 cm away (f).
- **Technical description** (5.2.2.1): precautions against harm from disturbances, the compliance levels reached, and what the hospital must do to keep it that way for the equipment's life (c). Equipment with a radio transmitter must state its bands and power (5.2.2.4).
- **Environments** (8.9, Figure 3): an ICU is a "professional healthcare facility environment". Areas next to active surgical diathermy and inside an MRI shielded room are "special environments" with stronger disturbances.
- **Nearby radios** (8.10, Table 9): medical equipment is tested against phones, Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz and 5 GHz) and Bluetooth held 30 cm away. A Wi-Fi tablet kept at least 30 cm from ICU equipment stays within what that equipment was tested to tolerate; closer is not covered.

**Relation to INNUTRIRE:** INNUTRIRE makes no hardware. Its software runs on Microsoft Surface tablets with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth radios, used at the bedside next to ventilators, monitors, infusion pumps and calorimeters, and on ordinary servers away from patients. These are IT and radio equipment with their own EMC and radio approvals (e.g. CISPR 32, and MCMC type approval for radios in Malaysia), so the standard probably does not apply to INNUTRIRE directly. Three things still carry over: the applicability decision must be recorded, electromagnetic causes (Wi-Fi interference or loss, tablet crash or reset, the tablet's own radio near ICU equipment) belong in the risk file, and users need guidance on radio distance and use environment. Hardware tests (clauses 6 to 9) are covered only by the applicability check EM-01.

Check IDs EM-01 to EM-03 keep the same meaning as earlier versions of this checklist, because cards on the Trello board are matched by ID. New checks get new numbers.

### EM-01 Applicability decision and equipment EMC approvals recorded
- **Applies to:** all (normally kept once, outside the repo or in the shared compliance repo)
- **Clause:** 1.1, 4.2, 7.1.3, 7.1.6
- **Look for:** a written decision on whether IEC 60601-1-2 applies to INNUTRIRE, with the reason (software on IT equipment, no medical electrical equipment made or connected), and a record naming the tablet and server models with their EMC approvals (e.g. CISPR 32 emissions, an immunity standard) and radio approvals (MCMC type approval), ideally the makers' declarations of conformity. If the decision is that the standard does apply, the record should point to the test plan and test report (clauses 6 and 9). Search `compliance_docs` for "does not apply" statements.
- **Met:** decision and approvals recorded. **Partial:** decision without the equipment approvals, or approvals without the decision. **Gap:** neither. **Outside repo** if kept in the technical file.
- **Severity if not met:** Low

### EM-02 Guidance on radio use next to ICU equipment
- **Applies to:** provisioning, tablet-app
- **Clause:** 5.2.1.1 c) and f), 8.10 (Table 9)
- **Look for:** Wi-Fi and Bluetooth settings in the provisioning scripts (`scripts/02-configure-wifi.ps1`, any Bluetooth or NFC switch-off), any Bluetooth or NFC use in the app, and written guidance for staff: keep the tablet at least 30 cm from ventilators, monitors, pumps and calorimeters (the distance those devices are tested for), don't rest it on or stack it with them, and follow the hospital's own rules on wireless devices.
- **Met:** radios limited to hospital Wi-Fi and guidance given to users. **Partial:** radios configured but no guidance. **Gap:** extra radios enabled without reason.
- **Severity if not met:** Low

### EM-03 No wired link to medical equipment
- **Applies to:** backend, tablet-app, provisioning
- **Clause:** 4.2, 8.8 (with IEC 60601-1 clause 16); Annex A rationale for 1.1
- **Look for:** the same signals as ES-02 (serial, USB, RS-232 or direct links, device drivers or vendor SDKs for calorimeters, ventilators or monitors), plus any setup where the tablet is charged from or mounted on a medical device's cart and shares its power outlet. Either makes INNUTRIRE's IT equipment part of a medical electrical system. Data that only travels over the hospital network is not a wired link, but its effects belong in EM-04.
- **Met:** no link (out of scope), or a link with a record that the IT equipment meets its own EMC standards and a risk decision on whether it could affect the medical device's safety. **Gap:** a link exists with no such record.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### EM-04 Electromagnetic causes are in the risk analysis
- **Applies to:** all (usually in the risk file in `compliance_docs`)
- **Clause:** 4.1, Annex F (F.3, F.5.1)
- **Look for:** risk file rows that name electromagnetic causes and link them to harm: Wi-Fi interference or loss in the ICU leading to a lost, partial or delayed record; a tablet or app crash or reset mid-entry; power dips or cuts at the server; and the tablet's own radio disturbing nearby medical equipment. Each should have a control, ideally a software one (offline cache, retry, integrity checks, saved drafts). If RM-11 already cites the same rows, reuse that evidence.
- **Met:** electromagnetic causes in the risk file with controls. **Partial:** Wi-Fi loss treated only as an IT availability issue with no harm link, or the tablet-as-transmitter direction missing. **Gap:** not considered.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### EM-05 Essential function is defined and fails safely when disturbed
- **Applies to:** tablet-app, backend
- **Clause:** 5.2.1.1 b), 8.1, Annex I (I.2, I.3.1)
- **Look for:** a statement of INNUTRIRE's essential function (e.g. the right REE value shown for the right patient) and of what users see if a network drop or crash interrupts it. In code: the tablet shows a clear offline or error state instead of a stale or partial value presented as current (`app/features/local_cache`, the API client's timeouts and error handling), entered data survives a crash or lost connection (drafts, local cache), and the backend saves a prediction completely or not at all.
- **Met:** function defined, and the app fails visibly without losing or corrupting data. **Partial:** handled in code but not defined or stated for users. **Gap:** a dropped connection or crash can silently lose entries or show an outdated value as current.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### EM-06 Use environment and EMC information given to users and hospital IT
- **Applies to:** tablet-app, provisioning (and any user or hospital deployment documentation)
- **Clause:** 5.2.1.1 a), d) and e), 5.2.2.1 c), 8.9 (Figure 3)
- **Look for:** instructions for use or a hospital deployment guide that state: the intended environment (professional healthcare facility, including ICU); places to avoid (inside an MRI shielded room, right next to active surgical diathermy); use only the tablet maker's charger and cables; and what hospital IT must keep doing (replace damaged chargers or tablets with approved ones, keep radio settings as provisioned).
- **Met:** stated in documents users or hospital IT actually get. **Partial:** only in developer docs, or environment stated without the exclusions. **Gap:** none.
- **Severity if not met:** Low
