# ISO 10993-1:2018 — Biological evaluation (key BC)

**Source:** checked against the full text of BS EN ISO 10993-1:2020, which is identical to ISO 10993-1:2018 (fifth edition, including the corrected version 2018-11). Clause numbers are exact.

**In plain words:** materials that touch a patient's body must not harm it (no irritation, allergy or poisoning). The first question is whether anything touches the patient at all, directly or indirectly. If nothing does, the standard does not apply, and that decision is simply written down. If something does, how deep the evaluation goes depends on what touches the body, where, and for how long, and existing knowledge about the materials often replaces testing.

**What the standard covers that matters for INNUTRIRE:**
- **Scope is patient contact** (1). It covers materials with direct or indirect contact with the patient's body, and with the user's body only when the device is meant to protect the user (gloves, masks). Germs and infection are excluded from this standard, so infection control is a hospital matter, not a biocompatibility one.
- **Direct, indirect and no contact** (3.6, 3.11, 3.16). Direct means the part itself touches body tissue. Indirect means a fluid or gas passes through the part before reaching the body. "Non-contacting" means neither.
- **Software counts as non-contacting** (5.2.1). Diagnostic software is named as an example of a device needing no biocompatibility information.
- **Touch screens and housings touched by hands** (5.2.2 a), Note). Parts of electronic equipment such as tablets, keyboards and touch screens that touch intact skin or ungloved hands need no further evaluation if they are made of materials commonly used in consumer products with similar contact.
- **Duration categories** (5.3). Limited means up to 24 h of total contact, prolonged up to 30 days, long-term beyond that, adding up repeated contacts (5.3.1, 5.3.3). Very brief contact (under about a minute) generally needs no testing (5.3.2). For intact skin and limited contact, Table A.1 (Annex A) lists only cytotoxicity (harm to cells), sensitisation (allergy) and irritation, and these can be covered by existing data or a written rationale rather than tests.
- **The decision flow** (4, Figure 1). The first box asks whether there is direct or indirect patient contact. If not, the standard does not apply. Testing is usually not needed when enough is already known (4.1, 4.4), and a decision to skip it must have a documented rationale (6.1, 7 d)).
- **Part of risk management** (4.1, Annex B.2). The evaluation sits inside the ISO 14971 risk process (this edition cites ISO 14971:2007, Annex I), is planned and documented, and names the person responsible. The conclusions are recorded by someone competent (7).
- **Cleaning and reprocessing affect materials** (4.8, B.3.1.2, B.4.1.5). Reusable devices are evaluated for the number of cleaning cycles the maker supports. Detergents, disinfectants and decontamination are listed as things that can change a material or leave residues.
- **Re-evaluate when things change** (4.7, 4.9, B.4.5.1). Biological safety is kept over the whole life of the product. A change of material, supplier, processing (including cleaning), storage or intended use, or any sign of a skin reaction in use, triggers a new look.

**Relation to INNUTRIRE:** INNUTRIRE is software, which the standard itself names as non-contacting. The only physical item at the bedside is an off-the-shelf Microsoft Surface tablet. Staff touch it with their hands (not in scope: the tablet does not protect them, and 5.2.2 Note covers it anyway). A patient's skin might touch it briefly by accident, which would be intact skin, very brief contact, consumer-product materials. The parts that do touch patients (calorimeter mask, canopy, ventilator circuit) belong to the calorimeter makers. So the realistic outcome is a recorded "does not apply / no patient contact" decision in the risk file, plus cleaning guidance for the shared tablets.

Check IDs BC-01 to BC-03 keep the same meaning as earlier versions of this checklist, because cards on the Trello board are matched by ID. New checks get new numbers.

### BC-01 Non-applicability statement recorded
- **Applies to:** all (normally kept once for the whole system, in the risk file or the shared compliance repo; a repo may link to it)
- **Clause:** 1, 4.1, 5.2.1, Figure 1 (first decision), 6.1 (rationale documented)
- **Look for:** a written decision, e.g. in `compliance_docs` or the risk management file, that INNUTRIRE has no direct or indirect patient contact and so ISO 10993-1 does not apply. A good record gives the reasons: software is non-contacting (5.2.1); the tablet is an off-the-shelf consumer device touched by staff hands (5.2.2 a), Note); any patient skin contact is incidental, intact skin and very brief (5.3.2); patient-contacting calorimeter parts belong to their makers. It should name who made the decision and when (4.1), and say it will be revisited if the hardware, accessories or intended use change (4.9).
- **Met:** decision recorded with reasons. **Partial:** a one-line "not applicable" with no reasons, or reasons with no owner. **Gap:** nothing found. **Outside repo** if kept in a technical file outside the repos; ask for it.
- **Severity if not met:** Low

### BC-02 No patient-contacting accessory specified
- **Applies to:** provisioning, infrastructure, tablet-app
- **Clause:** 5.1, 5.2, 5.3 (categorisation by nature and duration of contact)
- **Look for:** anything that would move INNUTRIRE into a contact category. In docs, setup scripts or app screens: mounting the tablet on the patient or so it rests against the patient; INNUTRIRE-supplied sensors, straps, covers, styluses or other parts that touch the patient; a workflow where patients or relatives handle the tablet (for example signing consent on screen). Brief patient handling of the tablet is intact-skin, limited contact and is covered by the consumer-materials note (5.2.2 a), Note), but the decision in BC-01 should mention it.
- **Met:** nothing found, or any such part or workflow is categorised and justified in the BC-01 record. **Partial:** patients handle the tablet by design but the BC-01 record doesn't say so. **Gap:** a part that touches patients is specified with no evaluation.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### BC-03 Cleaning and disinfection of shared tablets
- **Applies to:** provisioning, tablet-app (and any user or deployment guide given to hospitals)
- **Clause:** 4.8, 4.9 b), B.3.1.2, B.4.1.5 (cleaning agents can change materials or leave residues). Infection control itself is outside this standard (1), so this is about the tablet and case surviving disinfection, not about germs.
- **Look for:** guidance for hospitals on cleaning tablets used at the ICU bedside: which disinfectants are compatible with the tablet and its case (following the tablet and case makers' cleaning guidance), letting the surface dry before use, and replacing a case or screen protector that cracks, goes sticky or discolours. A case chosen because it tolerates hospital disinfectants also counts.
- **Met:** documented in a guide the hospitals receive. **Partial:** cleaning mentioned without compatible agents or a reference to the makers' guidance. **Gap:** nothing, where the repo holds the hospital guide. **Outside repo** if the hospital's own procedure covers it; ask the team.
- **Severity if not met:** Low
