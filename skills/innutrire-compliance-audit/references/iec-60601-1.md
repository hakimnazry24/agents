# IEC 60601-1:2005+A1:2012 (edition 3.1) — Basic safety and essential performance (key ES)

**Source:** checked against the full text of IEC 60601-1 edition 3.1 (2005 plus Amendment 1:2012). Clause numbers are exact for that edition. Edition 3.2 (Amendment 2:2020) exists but was not read; recheck the clause numbers if that text becomes available.

**In plain words:** medical electrical equipment must not shock, burn or otherwise harm anyone, and must keep doing the part of its clinical job whose loss would be dangerous (its "essential performance"). Most of the standard is hardware testing. The parts that still reach a software-only product are: deciding whether the standard applies at all, naming the essential performance, the extra software development rules for programmable systems (Clause 14), how the software connects to a hospital network, how it identifies its version, what its documents tell users, and what happens when non-medical equipment sits next to the patient.

**What the standard covers that matters for INNUTRIRE:**
- **Scope depends on the definitions** (1.1, 3.63, 3.64, 3.90). "ME equipment" is electrical equipment with a part that touches the patient, or that transfers energy to or from the patient, used for diagnosis, treatment or monitoring. Software on its own is not ME equipment. Software is covered only as part of a "PEMS" (programmable electrical medical system: ME equipment or an ME system that contains a computer). An "ME system" is a set of equipment, at least one item being ME equipment, joined by a "functional connection" or a shared power strip.
- **A functional connection is not only a cable** (3.33). Any link that carries signals, data, power or substances counts, so a wireless data link between the tablet and a ventilator or calorimeter would also create an ME system. Plugging into a wall socket does not.
- **Hardware clauses can be closed by design** (4.2.3.1 c and its note). Where the design means a hazard the standard names cannot arise (no applied part, no mains part made by INNUTRIRE), no further risk assessment of that hazard is needed, but the reason should be written down. This covers power supply (4.10), insulation and leakage (8), mechanical (9), heat and fire (11, 13), construction (15) and similar clauses.
- **Risk management must follow ISO 14971** (4.2, 4.2.2). Judged by the RM checks; not repeated here.
- **Essential performance must be identified** (3.27, 4.3). The maker lists the clinical functions needed for the intended use, sets limits between fully working and total loss (in normal use and when one thing fails), judges the risk if performance falls past those limits, and calls it essential performance when that risk is unacceptable. Then controls are added and their checks named.
- **Clause 14 adds software process rules** when a programmable part provides basic safety or essential performance (14.1). Life cycle, problem resolution, verification and modification (14.4, 14.5, 14.10, 14.12) largely repeat IEC 62304 and are judged by the SW checks. What Clause 14 adds: requirements that mark essential performance and risk controls (14.7), architecture choices (14.8), a validation plan referenced in the risk plan (14.3) and run by someone independent of the design team (14.11), and software-specific hazards such as unavailable, wrong, late or tampered data and failing networks (14.6.1). When 14.2 to 14.13 apply, IEC 62304 clauses 4.3, 5, 7, 8 and 9 also apply.
- **Network connection (14.13) applies whether or not the rest of Clause 14 does.** If the software runs on a hospital network the maker did not validate, the maker must tell the hospital the purpose of the connection, the network characteristics and configuration needed, security specifications, the information flow, the hazardous situations if the network fails, and that changes to the network can bring new risks the hospital must assess (the hospital-side process is IEC 80001-1).
- **Software must carry a unique version identifier** available to service staff (7.2.2), and the instructions for use and technical description need their own version or issue date (7.9.2.19, 7.9.3.1).
- **Accompanying documents** (7.9) are the instructions for use and the technical description: intended use, needed training, use-environment limits, warnings, a list of system and error messages with what to do, range and accuracy of displayed values, and essential performance information.
- **ME systems** (16) require non-medical equipment used with ME equipment to meet its own IT safety standard (16.1), documents listing which parts may be used in the patient environment and warnings about extra power strips (16.2), and no hazardous situation from losing and regaining power, other than loss of function (16.8). The "patient environment" is the space where the patient can touch the equipment or touch someone who touches it, at least about 1.5 m around the patient (3.79, Annex A).

**Relation to INNUTRIRE:** INNUTRIRE is software on off-the-shelf Microsoft Surface tablets and ordinary servers, which follow IT equipment safety standards (IEC 62368-1, earlier IEC 60950-1). It has no part that touches the patient, makes no hardware and has no cable link to the calorimeters, so it is most likely outside the scope of this standard. That decision has to be written down with reasons (ES-01 to ES-04). The tablets are still used at the ICU bedside next to ventilators and monitors, and the software's output steers feeding, so the software-relevant parts (ES-05 to ES-12) are worth meeting as good practice and are largely also asked for by ISO 14971, IEC 62304 and IEC 80001-1. If the applicability statement excludes a clause with a stated reason, mark the matching check `Not applicable` and cite the statement.

Check IDs ES-01 to ES-04 keep the same meaning as earlier versions of this checklist, because cards on the Trello board are matched by ID. New checks get new numbers.

### ES-01 Non-applicability justification recorded
- **Applies to:** all (normally kept once for the whole system in the shared compliance repo; a repo may link to it)
- **Clause:** 1.1, 3.63, 3.64, 3.90 (also 4.2.3.1 c)
- **Look for:** a written statement on whether IEC 60601-1 applies, giving the reasons against the definitions: INNUTRIRE is software only, has no part that touches the patient and transfers no energy to or from the patient, and is not specified by INNUTRIRE as part of any ME equipment or ME system. It should name the tablet and server models and their IT safety approvals (IEC 62368-1 or IEC 60950-1, CE or similar marks), list the hardware clause groups closed by design (power supply 4.10, electrical 8, mechanical 9, temperature and fire 11 and 13, construction 15) with one line each, and say which parts are still applied as good practice (4.3, 7.2.2, 7.9, 14, 16). Check `compliance_docs` and the repo READMEs.
- **Met:** statement with reasons and named hardware approvals. **Partial:** a one-line "does not apply" with no reasons, or no hardware approvals named. **Gap:** nothing. **Outside repo** if kept in the technical file and not linked.
- **Severity if not met:** Low

### ES-02 No cable link to medical electrical equipment
- **Applies to:** backend, tablet-app, provisioning, infrastructure
- **Clause:** 3.33, 3.64, 16.1
- **Look for:** code or config that exchanges data with a calorimeter, ventilator, monitor or feeding pump by any route, wired or wireless: `pyserial`, `serial.Serial`, `COM` ports, USB or Bluetooth device access, HL7 or vendor SDKs, device IP addresses in inventories or firewall rules, or file imports from a device's export folder set up as an automatic link. Manual typing of calorimeter readings by a nurse is not a functional connection.
- **Met:** no such link (the ME system clause stays out of scope). **Gap:** a link exists with no ME system assessment.
- **Severity if not met:** High

### ES-03 No INNUTRIRE-specific hardware
- **Applies to:** provisioning, infrastructure
- **Clause:** 1.1, 3.63 (also 16.2, 16.9.2.1 if a power strip is supplied)
- **Look for:** custom hardware, enclosures, power supplies, chargers, docking stations, bedside mounts or power strips specified or supplied as part of INNUTRIRE, in docs, purchase lists or provisioning scripts. Standard tablet accessories bought off the shelf and used as their maker intends do not count.
- **Met:** none (out of scope). **Gap:** custom or supplied hardware with no assessment.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### ES-04 Software in the essential performance chain
- **Applies to:** backend, tablet-app
- **Clause:** 14.1, 14.13 (also 4.3)
- **Look for:** whether INNUTRIRE output is sent automatically to any medical electrical equipment, for example setting a feeding pump rate or writing into a ventilator or monitor, rather than shown to a clinician who decides. Look for outbound integrations, device APIs and any "auto-apply" setting.
- **Met:** output goes only to clinicians (the software is not part of a PEMS). **Gap:** automatic control of equipment with no Clause 14 assessment.
- **Severity if not met:** High

### ES-05 Essential performance identified
- **Applies to:** all (usually in the risk file; backend and tablet-app hold the evidence for limits)
- **Clause:** 3.27, 4.3
- **Look for:** a record that lists INNUTRIRE's clinical functions and decides for each whether it is essential performance. Likely candidates: the REE estimate is within a stated error of calorimetry for the intended population; the energy target (injury factor) is calculated correctly; the result is attached to the right patient; a result is available at the bedside when needed. For each: the limits between fully working and total loss, in normal use and when one thing fails (server down, network down, wrong model file, out-of-range input); the risk if performance falls past them, taking backups into account (predictive equations, clinical judgement); the decision; the controls; and how each control is verified.
- **Met:** functions listed, limits set, decision and controls recorded. **Partial:** functions or accuracy figures stated but no limits or decision. **Gap:** none. **Outside repo** if kept elsewhere; ask for it.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### ES-06 Clause 14 applicability decided and its extra records exist
- **Applies to:** all (usually in the shared compliance repo)
- **Clause:** 14.1, 14.2, 14.3, 14.7, 14.8
- **Look for:** a recorded decision on whether the software provides basic safety or essential performance (if a wrong REE can cause harm, it likely does). Where Clause 14 is applied: requirements that mark which items are essential performance and which are risk controls (14.7); an architecture that states how risk controls are allocated to components, failure modes, protection against foreseeable misuse and the network specification (14.8); the risk plan pointing to the validation plan (14.3); and these documents kept in or linked from the risk file under document control (14.2). Life cycle, problem resolution, verification and modification are judged by the SW checks; do not score them twice.
- **Met:** decision recorded, and if Clause 14 applies, requirements and architecture carry the markings above. **Partial:** requirements or architecture exist but do not mark essential performance or risk controls. **Gap:** no decision and no markings.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### ES-07 Validation by someone independent of the design team
- **Applies to:** all (usually outside the repo)
- **Clause:** 14.11 (also 14.3)
- **Look for:** a validation plan covering essential performance and checks for unintended behaviour, its methods and results, and the person responsible for it not being on the design team, with the reason for the chosen level of independence and any working relationships with designers recorded. For INNUTRIRE this may be the field study comparing predicted REE with calorimetry, or a clinical acceptance test before a hospital goes live. Repo evidence: links to a validation report or protocol, or a release checklist step that names the validator.
- **Met:** plan, results and independent validator recorded. **Partial:** validation done but by the developers, or no written plan. **Gap:** none. **Outside repo** if kept elsewhere. **Not applicable** only if ES-06 records that Clause 14 does not apply and why.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### ES-08 Hospital network connection is specified for the hospital
- **Applies to:** backend, tablet-app, monitoring, provisioning, infrastructure
- **Clause:** 14.13 (also 14.6.1, 14.8 n)
- **Look for:** deployment or installation docs a hospital's IT team would get, stating: why the tablets and server connect to the network; the network characteristics and configuration needed (Wi-Fi, static IPs, ports, bandwidth, the Tailscale tunnel to Moniteer); security specifications (authentication, encryption, firewall rules); the information flow and routing (tablet to server, server to Moniteer); the hazardous situations if the network fails or is slow (no prediction at the bedside, unsynced offline data, stale results); and a notice that connecting other equipment to that network, or changing, updating or removing items on it, can bring new risks the hospital must assess. Check `README`, `docs/`, Ansible role docs and tablet setup docs.
- **Met:** all items written for the hospital. **Partial:** network details exist only as scripts or developer notes, or the failure hazards and change notice are missing. **Gap:** none.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### ES-09 Software version is uniquely identified
- **Applies to:** backend, tablet-app, monitoring
- **Clause:** 7.2.2, 7.9.2.19, 7.9.3.1
- **Look for:** a unique version for each released part (tablet app, server, AI model) that service staff can read without opening code: an About screen, a version endpoint such as `/api/version`, Moniteer showing each site's versions, version stored with each prediction. Also a version or issue date on the instructions for use and the technical description.
- **Met:** every released part shows a unique version and the documents carry one. **Partial:** some parts only, or no version on the documents. **Gap:** no way to tell which version is running.
- **Severity if not met:** Low

### ES-10 Instructions for use and technical description
- **Applies to:** tablet-app, backend
- **Clause:** 7.9.1, 7.9.2.1, 7.9.2.2, 7.9.2.9, 7.9.2.10, 7.9.3.1
- **Look for:** documents users and hospital IT actually get (user guide, in-app help, hospital handover pack) that state: intended use and contraindications (patients it must not be used on); the training users need; where it may and may not be used; all warnings in one place; how to operate each screen; a list of system and error messages that are not self-explanatory, with likely cause and what to do; the range and accuracy of displayed values (REE, energy target, scores); essential performance information; and a warning against unauthorised modification. Language suited to nurses and dietitians.
- **Met:** both documents exist and cover these items. **Partial:** a user guide exists but lacks the message list, accuracy figures or warnings section, or exists only as developer docs. **Gap:** none.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### ES-11 Tablet use in the patient environment is assessed
- **Applies to:** provisioning, tablet-app (usually a deployment or site-setup document)
- **Clause:** 3.79, 16.1, 16.2 (also 16.9.2.1)
- **Look for:** a record that the tablet enters the patient environment (within about 1.5 m of the patient) and how that is made safe: the tablet and its charger meet their IT safety standard; whether tablets are charged at the bedside or away from it; that they must not be plugged into a ventilator's or monitor's power outlet or into a loose power strip near the patient; cleaning and disinfection between patients that the tablet maker allows; and the permitted environmental conditions. Radio interference is covered by the EM checks.
- **Met:** assessed and written into site setup or user documents. **Partial:** some points only, e.g. cleaning but not charging. **Gap:** none. **Not applicable** if the tablets are documented as never used within the patient environment.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### ES-12 Losing power or connection leaves no hazardous situation
- **Applies to:** backend, tablet-app
- **Clause:** 16.8 (by analogy; it binds ME systems), 4.3, 14.6.1
- **Look for:** evidence that a tablet battery running out, a server power cut or a network drop only stops the function and does not corrupt or silently lose data or show a stale result as current: unfinished entries saved as drafts, a prediction and its record saved in one database transaction, offline cache that syncs without duplicates or loss, clear on-screen status when the server is unreachable, recovery after restart (Docker restart policy, database integrity). Tests that simulate these failures count strongly.
- **Met:** handled in code and tested. **Partial:** handled in code but untested, or only some failures covered. **Gap:** a failure can lose or corrupt data or show a stale result without warning.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium
