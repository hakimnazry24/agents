# ISO 13485:2016 — Quality management system (key QM)

**Source:** checked against the full text of ISO 13485:2016 (third edition). Clause numbers are exact.

**In plain words:** the organisation's rulebook. Written procedures say how INNUTRIRE is designed, built, released, installed and supported. Records then prove each step was done. The standard does not test the product itself. It checks that the team works in a controlled, repeatable way, and it holds the evidence produced for all the other standards.

**What the standard covers that matters for INNUTRIRE:**
- **Software counts, and clauses can be excluded only with a reason.** Software is a product type (3.15), and the rules for devices also apply to services such as support (Clause 1). A requirement in Clauses 6, 7 or 8 that cannot apply can be left out, but the quality manual must say which ones and why (Clause 1, 4.2.2). Examples are sterile, implantable and cleanliness rules.
- **Software used to run the quality system must be validated.** Validation here means showing, with a record, that a tool does its job correctly for this use. Three clauses ask for it, each scaled to the risk: software used in the quality system (4.1.6), in production and service (7.5.6), and for monitoring and measuring (7.6). For INNUTRIRE that means CI, release and packaging tools, Ansible, Moniteer, Trello and AI coding assistants.
- **Documents and records have rules** (4.2.4, 4.2.5). Documents need approval before use, a visible revision status, and old versions kept. Records must be kept for at least the device's lifetime, and never less than two years after release. Changes to a record must stay visible. Confidential health information in records must be protected.
- **One file per device ties it all together** (4.2.3). It holds or points to the description, intended use, labelling, specifications, how the device is built and distributed, monitoring, installation and servicing. A separate design and development file (7.3.10) holds the design records and change records.
- **Full design controls** (7.3.2–7.3.10): a plan, approved inputs, outputs with acceptance criteria, dated reviews with named participants, verification and validation plans with acceptance criteria and a reason for the sample size, transfer to production, and controlled changes.
- **Validation runs on the real product and finishes before release** (7.3.7). It uses representative product, which for software means the actual release build. Clinical evaluation is part of it. A device used in a clinical evaluation, such as the field study, does not count as released to the customer.
- **A change must be checked against product already delivered** (7.3.9), e.g. the hospitals still running an older version.
- **Suppliers include free software** (3.16, 7.4). "Purchased product" needs no payment, so open-source libraries, GitHub, Tailscale and the cloud host are suppliers. Checks must be scaled to risk. Where it applies, suppliers should agree to warn of changes before making them (7.4.2). Any supplier change must be judged for its effect on the device (7.4.3).
- **A record for each device or batch, and for each installation** (7.5.1, 7.5.3, 7.5.8, 7.5.9). For software, a batch is one release build. The record shows what was built, who approved it and where it went. The person who authorised the release must be recorded (8.2.6).
- **Measuring equipment must be calibrated** (7.6). This covers the calorimeters (made by other companies) that give the gold-standard REE.
- **After release:** collect feedback (8.2.1), handle complaints (8.2.2), report to authorities where the law requires it (8.2.3), and act on problems found after delivery, including advisory notices (8.3.3). An advisory notice is a message telling users what to do about a problem with a delivered device. Fix the cause of problems, not just the symptom (8.5.2), and prevent likely problems before they happen (8.5.3).

**Relation to INNUTRIRE:** many engineering habits already exist: git, pull-request review, automated tests, release and promote workflows, version numbers stored with each prediction, an audit log and Trello. The usual gap is that they are not written down as approved procedures, their records are not kept long enough, and organisation-level documents are missing: the quality manual, supplier list, tool validation and device file. Most of these documents belong in `~/coderepo/compliance_docs` or outside the repos. Each repo can still show the practice and point to the document.

Check IDs QM-01 to QM-10 keep the same meaning as earlier versions of this checklist, because cards on the Trello board are matched by ID. New checks get new numbers.

### QM-01 Documented procedures
- **Applies to:** all (normally kept once in the shared compliance repo; each repo may link to them)
- **Clause:** 4.1.1, 4.1.3, 4.2.1 (c, d)
- **Look for:** written, approved procedures for the processes the standard requires: design and development (7.3.1), design changes (7.3.9), document and record control (4.2.4, 4.2.5), software tool validation (4.1.6), purchasing (7.4.1), installation (7.5.3), identification and traceability (7.5.8, 7.5.9), feedback and complaints (8.2.1, 8.2.2), nonconforming product (8.3) and corrective and preventive action (8.5.2, 8.5.3). Search `compliance_docs/`, `docs/`, `CONTRIBUTING*`, `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, runbooks and PR templates. Developer guidance such as `CLAUDE.md` shows the practice but is not an approved procedure.
- **Met:** approved procedures cover the required processes. **Partial:** informal guidance only, or only some processes covered. **Gap:** none.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### QM-02 Document control
- **Applies to:** all
- **Clause:** 4.2.4
- **Look for:** a rule for how quality documents are controlled, and signs it is followed:
  - each document has an owner and is approved before use, e.g. merged through a reviewed pull request with a named approver;
  - the revision status is visible (git history, a version or date in the document);
  - the current version is the one people use, e.g. links point to `main`, not to copies;
  - outside documents the team relies on are listed with their version, e.g. the standards and calorimeter manuals;
  - a set period for keeping obsolete versions, at least as long as the records made from them.
- **Met:** documents are versioned, approved through review with a named owner, and the retention of old versions is defined. **Partial:** versioned only, or approval happens but owners and retention are not defined. **Gap:** none.
- **Severity if not met:** Low

### QM-03 Records are kept, protected and retained
- **Applies to:** all
- **Clause:** 4.2.5
- **Look for:** a rule for which records are kept, where, for how long and how they are protected, and whether the tools actually keep them. Records include CI run history, test reports, pull-request reviews, release notes, deployment logs, the audit log and Moniteer snapshots. Check for:
  - a retention period of at least the device lifetime and never less than two years after release. GitHub Actions logs and artifacts expire after 90 days by default, so CI evidence needs exporting or a longer setting.
  - changes to a record stay visible: soft delete, the append-only audit log, git history;
  - confidential health information in records is protected: access control, encryption, no patient data in CI logs or exported reports (ties to CS-05, CS-08);
  - records can be found again: release tags linked to CI runs, backups that can be restored.
- **Met:** records kept with a defined retention that the tools honour, and health information protected. **Partial:** records exist but retention is undefined or shorter than two years, or protection is unclear. **Gap:** none.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### QM-04 Design and development planning and inputs
- **Applies to:** backend, tablet-app, monitoring
- **Clause:** 7.3.1, 7.3.2, 7.3.3
- **Look for:**
  - **plan:** a design plan that names the stages, the review at each stage, the verification, validation and transfer work, who is responsible, how outputs will be traced back to inputs, and the resources and skills needed. It must be kept up to date as work goes on (ties to SW-02).
  - **inputs:** written design inputs covering functional, performance, usability and safety requirements, plus the applicable regulations and standards, the outputs of risk management and lessons from earlier versions (ties to SW-03). The inputs must be reviewed and approved. Each requirement should be clear, testable and not in conflict with others.
- **Met:** both, with approved inputs. **Partial:** one of them, or inputs that are unapproved or not testable. **Gap:** neither.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### QM-05 Design review, verification and validation
- **Applies to:** backend, tablet-app, monitoring
- **Clause:** 7.3.4, 7.3.5, 7.3.6, 7.3.7
- **Look for:**
  - **review:** design reviews at planned stages, with a record of the version reviewed, the participants and the date. Branch protection and PR reviews cover code review; a stage review needs its own record.
  - **verification:** a verification plan with methods and acceptance criteria, and test results kept as records (ties to SW-05, SW-06, SW-07).
  - **validation:** a validation plan with acceptance criteria and a reason for the sample size, e.g. the 300-patient field study. It must run on the actual release build, with the reason for choosing that build recorded. A clinical evaluation is part of it, and validation must be finished before general release to hospitals.
  - **outputs:** design outputs that state acceptance criteria and the characteristics essential for safe use, e.g. accepted input ranges and model limits.
- **Met:** all four, with records. **Partial:** some, e.g. code review and tests but no validation plan or stage reviews. **Gap:** none.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### QM-06 Control of design changes
- **Applies to:** all
- **Clause:** 7.3.9
- **Look for:** every change is identified, judged for how much it matters (function, performance, usability, safety, regulatory status), then reviewed, verified, validated where needed and approved before it is released. The review should consider the effect on versions already delivered, such as the hospitals that will not get the new release, and on the risk file. Evidence includes a PR template with impact and risk questions, PR-title rules, conventional commits, a changelog, required reviewers, CI that must pass, and a promote workflow with approval.
- **Met:** all of these, with the impact and risk review recorded. **Partial:** some, or changes reviewed but impact on delivered versions and risk not considered. **Gap:** direct pushes to `main`, or released changes with no record.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### QM-07 Software tools used to build, release and run the quality system are validated
- **Applies to:** all
- **Clause:** 4.1.6, 7.5.6, 7.6 (last paragraph)
- **Look for:** a list of the software used to build, test, package, deploy, monitor and keep quality records, e.g. GitHub Actions, release tooling, PyInstaller, Docker, Ansible, the provisioning scripts, Moniteer, Trello and AI coding assistants. For each tool the list should give its intended use, its risk if it fails, the validation done before first use, and what triggers revalidation, such as a version upgrade. Validation work should match the risk. Where the output of a build or deploy step is not checked afterwards, the step itself needs validating (7.5.6). An example is a packaged tablet build with no smoke test, meaning a quick run to confirm it starts and works. Moniteer's comparison of predicted and gold-standard REE counts as monitoring software under 7.6.
- **Met:** a tool list with risk, validation evidence and revalidation triggers. **Partial:** a tool list only, or reasons given without evidence. **Gap:** none.
- **Severity if not met:** Low

### QM-08 Supplier control for third-party software and services
- **Applies to:** all
- **Clause:** 7.4.1, 7.4.2, 7.4.3, 4.1.5
- **Look for:** an approved supplier list with selection criteria scaled to risk. Suppliers include hosting and tunnel services (GitHub, Tailscale, the cloud host), tablet and OS makers, container base images, major libraries (SOUP, ties to SW-09) and calorimeter makers. Also check for:
  - a plan for monitoring and re-checking suppliers, with records;
  - where possible, an agreement that the supplier warns before changes, or a written quality agreement for outsourced work (4.1.5);
  - checks on what is received: pinned versions and lock files, hash checks, CI run on dependency updates;
  - a record that each supplier change was judged for its effect on the device, e.g. a reviewed dependency-bump PR.
- **Met:** suppliers assessed, monitored and verified, with records. **Partial:** listed only, or version pinning without an assessment. **Gap:** none.
- **Severity if not met:** Low

### QM-09 Feedback, complaints and corrective action
- **Applies to:** all
- **Clause:** 8.2.1, 8.2.2, 8.5.2, 8.5.3
- **Look for:**
  - **feedback:** a written route for feedback from hospitals and field staff that also feeds into risk management (8.2.1).
  - **complaints:** a complaint procedure with responsibilities for recording, deciding whether it is a complaint, investigating (or recording why not), deciding whether to report it and deciding on a fix (8.2.2). A complaint is any report of a fault in a released device. During the field study the device is not yet released (7.3.7), but the route should be ready.
  - **corrective action:** bugs fixed at their cause. Look for tickets with a root-cause note, a regression test, and a check that the fix worked and did not harm safety (8.5.2).
  - **preventive action:** likely problems found before they happen, e.g. trends from Moniteer or audit findings acted on (8.5.3).
- **Met:** a documented loop with records. **Partial:** bug tracking only, without root cause or complaint handling. **Gap:** none. **Outside repo** for the Trello process unless the repo links to it.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### QM-10 Medical device file and design and development file
- **Applies to:** all (normally one index in the shared compliance repo)
- **Clause:** 4.2.3, 7.3.10
- **Look for:** one index, per device or device family, that holds or points to:
  - a general description, the intended use and the labelling, including instructions for use;
  - the product specifications, how it is built, packaged and distributed, and the monitoring procedures;
  - installation requirements and servicing procedures, where they apply.

  Also a design and development file that points to the design records and the change records. The two can share one index if both parts are clearly there.
- **Met:** the index exists and its links work. **Partial:** an index that leaves out major parts. **Gap:** none.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### QM-11 Quality manual with scope and justified non-applicable clauses
- **Applies to:** all (usually in the shared compliance repo or outside the repo)
- **Clause:** 4.1.1, 4.2.2, Clause 1
- **Look for:** a quality manual that:
  - states the scope of the quality system and the organisation's role, e.g. manufacturer;
  - lists or points to the procedures and describes how the processes connect;
  - names every requirement in Clauses 6–8 that does not apply, with a reason.

  For INNUTRIRE the likely exclusions are the sterile, implantable, cleanliness and contamination clauses (6.4.2, 7.5.2, 7.5.5, 7.5.7, 7.5.9.2). Another is checking the device while connected to other medical devices (7.3.6, 7.3.7): the calorimeters are not linked by cable or data. A clause that is skipped without a written reason counts as not met.
- **Met:** a manual with scope and justified exclusions. **Partial:** a scope statement or an exclusion list without reasons. **Gap:** none. **Outside repo** if kept elsewhere; ask for it.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### QM-12 Each release and each installation is identified, approved and recorded
- **Applies to:** all
- **Clause:** 7.5.1, 7.5.3, 7.5.8, 7.5.9.1, 8.2.6
- **Look for:**
  - **release:** each release build has a unique version and a fixed build, e.g. a git tag, image digest or file hash. Its test status is recorded, and so is the name of the person who approved it, e.g. a release workflow with an environment approval.
  - **installation:** written requirements for installation and an acceptance check for each server and tablet set up. Evidence is in the provisioning scripts, Ansible roles and a post-install checklist.
  - **record:** which version is installed at which hospital and on which tablet, and when.
  - **traceability:** each patient record can be traced to the software and model version that made it, e.g. `app_version`, `server_version`, `model_version` on predictions and `/api/version`.

  This overlaps CI-17 and the second half of the QM-03 card ("no record of each tablet or server set up").
- **Met:** releases identified and approved by a named person, installations checked and recorded per site, and outputs traceable to versions. **Partial:** some of these, e.g. versions stored but no per-site installation record. **Gap:** none.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### QM-13 Calorimeters used for gold-standard readings are identified and calibrated
- **Applies to:** backend (and the field study procedures outside the repo)
- **Clause:** 7.6 (a, c, and the paragraph on equipment found out of calibration)
- **Look for:** the gold-standard REE comes from calorimeters made by other companies and is used to validate the model. Look for:
  - which calorimeter took each reading: the gold-standard record holds `brand_type`, but check for a unit identifier as well;
  - a calibration or verification schedule and its records;
  - a rule for re-checking past readings if a unit is later found out of calibration.

  Calibration records are usually kept by the hospital or study team.
- **Met:** each reading traceable to a calibrated unit, with a rule for out-of-calibration findings. **Partial:** brand recorded but not the unit, or calibration records not linked. **Gap:** neither. **Outside repo** for the calibration records themselves.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium

### QM-14 Faulty releases are stopped, and field problems lead to action and notices
- **Applies to:** all
- **Clause:** 8.3.1, 8.3.2, 8.3.3, 8.2.3
- **Look for:**
  - **before delivery:** a build that fails its checks cannot reach hospitals, e.g. CI gates and promote rules. Releasing with a known defect needs a written reason and a named approver (8.3.2).
  - **after delivery:** a written procedure for problems found in the field. Evidence includes a way to find which sites run an affected version, a rollback path and a way to tell users, such as release notes for hospitals or an in-app message. It also needs a procedure for issuing advisory notices that can be used at any time (8.3.3).
  - **authorities:** a procedure for reporting adverse events to regulators where the law requires it (8.2.3).
  - **records:** what was wrong, how it was judged and what was done (8.3.1).
- **Met:** release gates plus documented field-action, advisory-notice and reporting procedures. **Partial:** release gates only. **Gap:** none. **Outside repo** for the procedures if kept elsewhere.
- **Severity if not met:** Medium
